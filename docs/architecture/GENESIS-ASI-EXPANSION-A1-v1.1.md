# IROKA GENESIS — Expanded ASI Architecture and A1 Acceptance Baseline

**Document ID:** GENESIS-ASI-A1-001  
**Version:** 1.1  
**Repository:** `akefadaka-git/iroka`  
**Architecture:** GENESIS — Artificial Genome and catastrophic self-reconstruction  
**System:** IROKA ZERO  
**Stage:** A1 — architecture, requirements and acceptance definition  
**Status:** Owner-review draft; not frozen; not an implementation claim  
**Governance:** Owner-governed, stage-gated; A5 deployment remains separate

> **Governing principle:** IROKA may become more capable and autonomous, but intelligence does not confer constitutional authority. Evolution, execution, recovery and promotion must remain governed, verifiable, recoverable and accountable.

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

GENESIS defines the architecture by which IROKA preserves its identity, approved Genome, knowledge, memory, capabilities, governance and operational continuity through ordinary failures and catastrophic loss. This expanded A1 amendment also establishes an advanced-intelligence capability horizon, including research intelligence, multimodal understanding, long-horizon planning, multi-agent systems and governed self-improvement.

AGI and ASI are aspirational capability horizons, not achieved-state claims. No design document, benchmark, simulation or successful component test proves that IROKA has achieved AGI or ASI.

This document is an architecture and acceptance proposal. It does not authorize implementation, merging, key ceremonies, external side effects, activation or deployment.

## 2. Architecture objectives

1. **Intelligence:** support modular reasoning, research, multimodal perception, planning, learning and specialized agents.
2. **Continuity:** preserve approved identity, Genome, artifacts, state, evidence and capability definitions.
3. **Governance:** preserve Owner authority, constitutional constraints and independent assurance.
4. **Evolution:** enable evidence-driven improvement without unauthorized self-modification.
5. **Recoverability:** reconstruct from independently verifiable artifacts and state.
6. **Interoperability:** prioritize Amharic and Ethiopian language/script requirements while supporting multilingual and cross-domain interoperability.

## 3. Constitutional invariants

### 3.1 Owner authority

The Owner retains the highest project-level authorization authority. IROKA must not manufacture or infer approval, grant itself permissions, replace trust roots, modify constitutional authority, promote an unapproved Genome or disable required assurance.

### 3.2 Separation of intelligence and authority

Intelligence, confidence, prediction accuracy, benchmark performance and computational capability do not confer authority. G03 can reason about an action; G07 can coordinate authorized delegation; G09 can perform permitted effects; G02 governs required authority; G11 provides assurance.

### 3.3 Governed self-improvement

IROKA may identify gaps, research improvements, generate proposals and test isolated candidates. It cannot unilaterally approve, activate, conceal or exempt a constitutional, security, trust, policy or capability change from required review.

### 3.4 Fail-safe degradation

Insufficient integrity, provenance, trust, authorization or recovery confidence requires reduced permissions or quarantine.

### 3.5 No recovery privilege escalation

Restoration must not revive revoked credentials, expired delegations, deleted data, prohibited capabilities or superseded/revoked Genome versions.

### 3.6 Independent verification

The system under recovery cannot be the sole authority that declares its recovery successful.

# Part I — Advanced intelligence capability architecture

## 4. Capability families

These are cross-cutting capability families mapped to the existing twelve chromosomes. They do not create new chromosomes or alter the authoritative Genome catalogue without a separately approved contract amendment.

### 4.1 Advanced reasoning and cognition (ARC)

Capabilities:
- Multi-step symbolic and neural reasoning.
- Mathematical, logical and formal reasoning.
- Causal inference and counterfactual analysis.
- Constraint satisfaction and optimization.
- Hypothesis generation, falsification and counterexample search.
- Probabilistic reasoning and calibrated uncertainty.
- Long-context synthesis and cross-domain integration.
- Self-critique and independent verification.

Ownership: primarily G03, supported by G04 and G11.

Acceptance:
- Reproducible evaluation tasks and disclosed benchmark limitations.
- Calibration, uncertainty and abstention evaluation.
- Adversarial and counterexample testing.
- Clear separation of evidence, inference, hypothesis and speculation.
- Reasoning output alone never triggers protected side effects.

### 4.2 Scientific discovery and research intelligence (SAI)

Capabilities:
- Literature and evidence synthesis.
- Research question and hypothesis generation.
- Experiment and simulation design.
- Mathematical/computational model construction.
- Reproducible analysis and anomaly detection.
- Contradiction detection and independent review.
- Research memory and evidence lineage.

Ownership: G03, G04, G05, G10 and G11.

Acceptance:
- Claims carry provenance and evidence classification.
- Simulation is distinguished from physical experimentation.
- Failed and contradictory results are retained.
- Reproducible research packages can be reconstructed.
- No fabricated sources, experiments or results.

### 4.3 Multimodal perception and world understanding (MPW)

Capabilities:
- Text, image, audio and video understanding.
- Spatial and temporal understanding.
- Document, diagram and sensor-stream interpretation through authorized adapters.
- Multilingual and cross-script processing.
- Provenance-preserving transformations.
- Ambiguity, authenticity and uncertainty detection.

Ownership: G06, supported by G03, G04 and G11.

Acceptance:
- Forged, malformed and oversized input rejection.
- Source provenance survives transformations.
- Cross-modal consistency and adversarial evaluation.
- Unsupported perception claims are explicitly limited.

### 4.4 Long-horizon planning and decision intelligence (LPI)

Capabilities:
- Hierarchical goal decomposition.
- Long-term project and resource planning.
- Dependency management and scenario analysis.
- Multi-objective optimization.
- Replanning under changed conditions.
- Risk-sensitive decision support and reversible execution plans.

Ownership: G03, G07, G08 and G09.

Acceptance:
- Plans identify measurable objectives, dependencies, uncertainty, stopping conditions and recovery actions.
- Irreversible/high-impact operations require the relevant human authorization.
- A long-term goal never implies unlimited authority.

### 4.5 Multi-agent intelligence (MAI)

Capabilities:
- Specialized research, coding, security, analysis, simulation and operations agents.
- Distinct agent identity and provenance.
- Bounded capability profiles and attenuated delegation.
- Parallel research, adversarial review and disagreement resolution.
- Shared knowledge under tenant/classification controls.
- Agent suspension, revocation and recovery.

Ownership: G07, G01, G02, G08, G09 and G11.

Acceptance:
- No agent elevates its own authority.
- Delegated scope cannot exceed the delegator's authority.
- Revoked agents cannot continue protected operations.
- Independence claims account for shared trust roots, operators and execution domains; majority vote alone is not independent assurance.

### 4.6 Continual learning and adaptation (CLA)

Capabilities:
- Approved supervised and retrieval-based learning.
- Evaluation-driven adaptation and continual learning.
- Knowledge consolidation and regression detection.
- Catastrophic-forgetting monitoring.
- Poisoning and data-quality detection.
- Learning provenance, version lineage and rollback.

Ownership: G05, G10 and G12.

Acceptance:
- Learning inputs have provenance and classification.
- Proposals are independently evaluated.
- No unapproved model or Genome mutation.
- Regression testing and accepted lineage are mandatory.
- Reversibility is provided where technically possible.

### 4.7 Software and systems engineering intelligence (SEI)

Capabilities:
- Code analysis and synthesis.
- Architecture and formal specification design.
- Test generation and coverage analysis.
- Vulnerability and supply-chain analysis.
- Build, migration and deployment planning.
- Reproducible development environments.
- Repository changes through explicitly authorized scopes.

Ownership: G03, G07, G08, G09, G11 and G12.

Acceptance:
- Generated changes are reviewed, tested and traceable.
- No direct production deployment without separate authorization.
- Supply-chain provenance is preserved.
- Security-critical modifications receive independent verification.

### 4.8 Computational self-model and introspection (CSI)

Capabilities:
- Inventory of available capabilities, models, adapters and versions.
- Runtime, resource and health awareness.
- Known-limitations registry.
- Confidence, uncertainty and contradiction monitoring.
- Failure detection and recovery-readiness assessment.

Ownership: G03, G11 and G12.

Acceptance:
- Capability claims derive from verified runtime evidence.
- Missing or experimental capabilities are not reported as active.
- Self-assessment never replaces independent assurance or bypasses governance.

### 4.9 Digital and physical-world interaction (DPI)

Capabilities:
- Authorized web, application and API interaction.
- Sandboxed computation and controlled file/data processing.
- Simulation environments.
- Future robotics and sensor adapters, subject to separate safety design.
- Physical-actuation policy gates, safe-state controls and emergency stop.

Ownership: G06, G07, G08, G09 and G11.

Acceptance:
- Least-privilege credentials and bounded scopes.
- Physical actions require separate safety engineering and authorization.
- Simulated permissions never imply real-world permissions.
- Emergency-stop and interlock mechanisms are independent of the intelligence process.

### 4.10 Multilingual and localized knowledge (MCK)

Capabilities:
- Amharic-first interaction and reasoning.
- Ethiopic script and Maale language support.
- Expansion to Ethiopian and African languages.
- Multilingual semantic alignment.
- Calendar, identity, document and geographic localization.
- Separation of cultural, legal and technical context.

Ownership: G03, G04, G05, G06 and G10.

Acceptance:
- Language quality is evaluated independently by language.
- Translation does not silently change legal or technical meaning.
- Corpus and localized data retain provenance and licensing.
- Cultural assumptions are identified rather than treated as universal.

## 5. Capability risk classes

| Class | Capability/action profile | Required governance |
|---|---|---|
| R0 | Read-only analysis and explanation | Authenticated access and ordinary audit |
| R1 | Reversible sandboxed operations | Bounded capabilities, quotas and audit |
| R2 | External digital actions | Explicit scoped authorization |
| R3 | Sensitive or high-impact operations | Human approval plus independent assurance |
| R4 | Physical, irreversible or critical-infrastructure actions | Separate safety authority, independent interlocks and human control |
| R5 | Constitutional, trust-root, self-modification or authority changes | Owner authorization, independent verification and staged activation |

Benchmark performance cannot automatically upgrade a capability's risk class.

## 6. Governed self-improvement lifecycle

1. Observe a verified performance, safety or reliability gap.
2. Form an improvement hypothesis with evidence.
3. Create a versioned change proposal.
4. Identify affected chromosomes, genes, contracts, data and dependencies.
5. Assess security, safety, privacy, legal and operational impacts.
6. Build an isolated candidate.
7. Run functional, adversarial, regression and recovery tests.
8. Obtain required independent review.
9. Obtain applicable Owner or delegated human approval.
10. Sign and preserve the accepted release and lineage.
11. Activate through a staged, reversible rollout.
12. Monitor and contain or roll back when acceptance conditions fail.

No autonomous transition from experiment to production is permitted.

## 7. Intelligence safety and assurance

Required safeguards include capability-specific evaluation; prompt-injection and untrusted-input defenses; poisoning detection; delegation and privilege-escalation tests; cybersecurity abuse resistance; privacy controls; dual-use review where relevant; sandboxing and quotas; independent containment and shutdown; human review for high-impact decisions; and incident learning.

The system must not conceal material failures, fabricate evidence, manipulate approvals or disable monitoring.

# Part III — Continuity and recovery

## 9. Six continuity layers

| Layer | Purpose | Required content |
|---|---|---|
| A — Immutable genetic root | Preserve accepted identity and Genome | Signed Genome, chromosome/gene catalogue, schemas, release lineage and trust evidence |
| B — Reproducible artifact vault | Preserve reconstructable software and models | Source, dependencies, build inputs, SBOM, schemas, migrations, tests and lawful model artifacts |
| C — Durable state continuity | Preserve authorized state | Memory, knowledge, workflows, learning records, audit, approvals and deletion state |
| D — Independent integrity anchors | Verify integrity independently | Witness receipts, hashes, signatures, retention proofs and custody evidence |
| E — Recovery control plane | Reconstruct safely | Isolated restore, artifact verification, state replay, quarantine and promotion gates |
| F — Geographic/administrative independence | Survive correlated failures | Three separately administered domains and at least one offline/disconnected copy |

## 10. Core-function continuity profiles

### CP0 — Integrity-only

- Verify accepted Genome identity, digest and trust evidence.
- Inspect and verify recovery packages.
- Perform offline deterministic checks.
- Preserve forensic evidence.
- No protected execution, model calls, agent delegation or automatic promotion.

### CP1 — Recovery-ready read-only

- All CP0 functions.
- Restore approved read-only knowledge and memory packages.
- Verify identity, authority and audit evidence.
- Rebuild derived indexes from approved sources.
- Coordinate bounded reconstruction.
- Keep protected execution disabled.

### CP2 — Degraded operations

- Operate verified available intelligence functions.
- Use approved local/deterministic fallbacks.
- Disable missing or unverified chromosome capabilities.
- Enable only validated agent identities and capabilities.
- Report degraded functions and provider substitutions.

### CP3 — Fully restored service

- All accepted functions for the restored Genome release.
- Trust, state and audit reconciled.
- Independent assurance completed.
- Explicit authorized promotion recorded.
- Enhanced monitoring and containment/rollback controls enabled.

CP0 and CP1 are mandatory minimum recovery capabilities. CP3 is never automatic.

## 11. Failure-domain architecture

- **RD-1:** primary operational domain.
- **RD-2:** independently administered regional recovery domain.
- **RD-3:** offline or disconnected continuity domain.

No single identity root, administrator, deployment pipeline or cloud account may control all three.

Failure domains to assess: process/service; host/storage; availability zone/region; account/control plane; identity/key custody; supply chain/build; data integrity; model/provider; and human/administrative.

Three buckets, machines or regions under one administrator do not prove independence. Actual providers, regions, operators, jurisdictions and custodians remain unselected pending review.

# Part IV — Data classes and recovery objectives

## 12. Data classification

| Class | Data | Proposed RPO |
|---|---|---|
| DC-0 | Constitutional Genome and accepted releases | 0 for accepted releases |
| DC-1 | Trust roots, revocation, Owner approvals and policies | 0 for accepted changes |
| DC-2 | Audit and security evidence | 0 for externally anchored protected transitions; ≤5 min for explicitly unanchored routine events |
| DC-3 | Durable memory and knowledge | ≤15 min |
| DC-4 | Workflow and operational state | ≤5 min |
| DC-5 | Derived indexes and caches | Rebuildable from authoritative records |
| DC-6 | Model artifacts and provider metadata | Depends on lawful ownership and provider availability |
| DC-7 | Temporary and in-flight state | ≤1 min for accepted task checkpoints |

Deletion records must be reapplied before recovered data becomes available. Restores must not resurrect deleted or revoked records. Deletion tombstones, retention expiry and cryptographic-erasure evidence must be part of recovery reconciliation.

## 13. Proposed RTO objectives

| Failure | Target RTO |
|---|---:|
| Process failure | ≤15 min |
| Host/storage failure | ≤4 h |
| Availability-zone failure | ≤8 h |
| Regional failure | ≤24 h |
| Primary account/control-plane loss | ≤24 h |
| Trust-root compromise | ≤72 h for an authorized new trust baseline |
| Model-provider outage | ≤1 h to enter approved degraded mode |

These are engineering targets, not measured guarantees. RPO is measured from the last accepted, recoverable and independently verifiable record—not backup-job time. RTO is measured from incident declaration to successful operation of the declared recovery profile. A restore failing integrity, authorization or tenant-isolation checks is not recovered service.

RPO 0 for accepted Genome releases means acceptance is not acknowledged until required independent copies and signatures are verified.

## 15. Mandatory recovery tests

- Offline signed Genome verification.
- Missing or modified artifact rejection.
- Missing gene schema rejection.
- Complete dependency closure and reproducible clean-room build.
- Restore from RD-2 and RD-3 without RD-1 credentials.
- Trust-root compromise and revocation.
- Memory, audit and workflow replay.
- Deletion tombstone enforcement.
- Cross-tenant isolation.
- Agent delegation and revocation.
- Unauthorized learning mutation rejection.
- Genome rollback rejection.
- Recovery-controller compromise containment.
- Regional failure and timed recovery.
- Independent attestation.
- Explicit promotion authorization.

Critical invariant violations have zero tolerance. No aggregate percentage can compensate for unauthorized promotion, acceptance of a revoked trust root, cross-tenant data exposure or resurrection of deleted data.

# Part VI — A1 gates and decisions

## 16. Stage gates

### A1 — Architecture and acceptance freeze
Complete authoritative chromosome/gene reconciliation; schema and artifact inventory; advanced-intelligence and safety architecture; recovery profiles; failure-domain design; data classes and RPO/RTO; traceability register; unresolved decisions; explicit Owner approval.

### A2 — Read-only technical reconciliation
Verify each requirement against repository evidence; establish exact branch, commit, PR and merge ancestry; reconcile contracts, schemas, code and tests; identify integration/security gaps; preserve existing work.

### A3 — Implementation
Implement only approved scope, separated by chromosome and acceptance requirement. Use synthetic tests without production keys or live external effects. Require exact-head verification.

### A4 — Independent verification
Functional/adversarial testing, reproducibility, recovery drills, measured RPO/RTO, independent security and safety review, evidence-backed closure.

### A5 — Deployment and activation
Separate approval required. No automatic promotion from A4.

## 17. Unresolved Owner decisions

1. Approve the advanced-intelligence capability families and mapping to the existing twelve chromosomes.
2. Approve CP0–CP3.
3. Approve three independent recovery domains.
4. Approve DC-0–DC-7 and proposed RPO/RTO targets.
5. Approve risk classes R0–R5.
6. Confirm proposal-driven self-improvement with independent verification and applicable human authorization.
7. Approve zero-resurrection deletion.
8. Defer provider, regional, physical-world and production-custody choices pending evidence and legal review.
9. Approve the complete A1 acceptance criteria before freeze.

## 18. A1 disposition

- Architecture and expanded capability horizon: defined as a proposal.
- Twelve-chromosome IGACMS authority: retained.
- G06/G07/G10: approved design responsibilities; executable implementation and recovery proof remain required.
- G12: existing IGACMS evolution foundation is reusable; GENESIS recovery integration remains required.
- Recovery: architecture and acceptance criteria defined; no end-to-end recovery capability claimed.
- Repository reconciliation: exact current repository evidence, schema closure and artifact closure remain required.
- A1 freeze: pending explicit Owner approval.
- Implementation, merge, activation and deployment: not authorized by this document.

**Final governing principle:** IROKA's intelligence may expand, but intelligence must never become its own constitutional authority. Every increase in capability must remain governed, evaluated, independently verifiable and recoverable.
